Authentic Pakistani Beef Nihari

Beef Nihari is a rich and comforting Pakistani slow-cooked stew known for its tender beef, warming spices and flavorful gravy. It is especially popular as a hearty breakfast or weekend family meal.

Prep Time: 20 minutes
Cook Time: 3–4 hours
Servings: 5–6

Ingredients

  • 1 kg beef shank or stewing beef, cut into large pieces
  • 4 tablespoons cooking oil or ghee
  • 1 large onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tablespoons wheat flour
  • 2 teaspoons red chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 teaspoon coriander powder
  • 1 teaspoon cumin powder
  • 1 teaspoon fennel seeds
  • 1 teaspoon salt, or to taste
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3–4 cloves
  • 3 cardamom pods
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 6 cups water, approximately

For Garnishing

  • Fresh coriander, chopped
  • Fresh ginger, cut into thin strips
  • Green chilies, sliced
  • Lemon wedges
  • Fried onions, optional

How to Make Beef Nihari

  1. Heat oil or ghee in a large heavy-bottomed pot. Add the sliced onion and cook until lightly golden.
  2. Add the ginger-garlic paste and cook for about one minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the beef pieces and cook over medium-high heat until the meat changes color and develops a little browning.
  4. Add chili powder, turmeric, coriander, cumin, fennel, salt and the whole spices. Stir well so the beef is coated with the spices.
  5. Pour in the water and bring the mixture to a gentle boil.
  6. Cover the pot and cook on low heat for about 3–4 hours, or until the beef becomes very tender and the gravy develops a rich flavor.
  7. In a separate bowl, mix the wheat flour with a little water to make a smooth paste. Slowly add it to the Nihari while stirring.
  8. Continue cooking for another 15–20 minutes until the gravy becomes slightly thick and silky.
  9. Taste and adjust the salt or spices if needed.
  10. Serve hot with fresh ginger, coriander, green chilies and lemon.

Tips for Better Nihari

  • Low and slow cooking helps the beef become tender and flavorful.
  • Beef shank works particularly well because it adds body to the gravy.
  • Add the flour gradually to avoid lumps.
  • Let the Nihari rest for a few minutes before serving.
  • Serve with naan, roti or fresh bread.

What to Serve With Nihari

Beef Nihari pairs beautifully with naan, khameeri roti or other fresh breads. Fresh ginger, green chilies, coriander and lemon add brightness to the rich gravy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make Nihari without beef shank?

Yes. Other suitable beef stewing cuts can be used, although cooking time may vary depending on the cut.

Can Nihari be prepared in advance?

Yes. Like many slow-cooked dishes, Nihari can develop even deeper flavor after resting. Store it safely in the refrigerator and reheat thoroughly before serving.

What is traditionally served with Nihari?

Nihari is commonly enjoyed with naan or other fresh bread and garnished with ginger, green chilies, coriander and lemon.
